The product at a glance

Three ways Privacy Center helps

Privacy Center helps your Salesforce team handle customer privacy requests, remove personal information you no longer need, and manage people's consent and communication preferences. You build reusable policies for requests and cleanup, keep selected information separately when it still needs to be retained, and track what happened when the work runs. That means less repeated manual work, less unnecessary personal data in everyday use, and a clearer way to show that you've followed through on your privacy decisions.

Three ways Privacy Center helps
Use What it helps you do
Data Subject Requests Give a person a copy of their data through DSAR, or apply the required masking or deletion policy for an RTBF request.
Proactive Organizational Data Management Find data the organization no longer needs, such as inactive Contacts. Mask selected personal details or delete records, and optionally keep a separate copy.
Consent Management Let people manage communication preferences through Preference Manager. Organize and manage consent centrally through Global Consent Manager.

Support for the three areas

Keep a separate copy when needed. CRM RTBF and Data Management policies can retain selected values in a separate store. Vignette 5 explains the store setup and how to view those records through Salesforce Connect.

Preserve records that must be kept. A record may need to remain unchanged during an investigation even when it normally qualifies for cleanup. Vignette 6 explains how to record that reason and configure the cleanup policy to skip it.

Decide what to improve. Agentforce scans help the organization identify missing classifications, gaps in policies, and other privacy issues. Use the findings to set priorities and choose the next action. Vignette 8 shows the dashboard and scans.

CRM RTBF and Data Management

Same actions, different ways to choose records

Both policies can mask or delete data and optionally keep a separate copy. RTBF processes records identified in a person's request. Data Management finds records using rules, such as "Contact is inactive".

Data Subject Requests · Vignette 1

1. DSAR / Portability

Overview

A Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) is a person asking an organization, “What information do you hold about me, and how are you using it?” The data subject is simply the person the information is about. Privacy laws such as the GDPR give individuals the right to access their personal information and obtain a copy. Privacy Center helps prepare that copy by collecting the Salesforce records and fields selected in a reusable Portability Policy.

Use case

A customer asks for a copy of their information. They have a Contact record, related Opportunities, support Cases, and Tasks. Rather than asking someone to reconstruct that picture for each request, the privacy team defines the records and fields to collect in a Portability Policy. It then runs the policy for the customer and reviews the resulting file. The next request starts with those agreed instructions, not another round of manual record gathering.

Build the reusable Portability Policy
  1. Open Privacy Center → Home → Portability Policies → Go to the DSAR Policy Manager in Setup. Choose New Policy and add Contact as the root.
  2. Select Contact, click Add Object, and add Opportunity Contact Role beneath it.
  3. Select that Opportunity Contact Role node. Click Add Object and add Opportunity beneath it.
  4. Select Contact again. Add Case and Task directly beneath Contact as separate branches.
  5. Select the fields to include on every object. Include the Task's Genesys Interaction ID when it is needed to retrieve external interaction data. Select at least one field on each object.
  6. Save and Activate the Portability Policy.
Run it for a person and review the export

The policy is ready. Now choose whose information to collect.

  1. Open Rachel's Contact and confirm the target ID and related records.
  2. From the active Portability Policy, choose Run, enter the Contact's 18-character ID, and submit.
  3. Open Home → Portability Log and locate the run by policy, subject, and time.
  4. After completion, open File URL and review the exported objects, records, and fields.
  5. Use the exported interaction IDs to retrieve any required external content and assemble the final response.

Data Subject Requests · Vignette 2

2. CRM Right to Be Forgotten

Overview

A Right to Be Forgotten (RTBF) request starts with a person asking an organization to remove their personal information. In Salesforce CRM, Privacy Center uses a reusable policy to define which records and fields to process and whether to mask selected values or delete records. The request identifies the person; the policy defines the treatment. Selected information can also be kept in a separate retention store when required.

Use case

A customer asks the organization to remove personal contact information it no longer needs. In this example, the privacy team applies a policy that replaces the Contact’s Email with a masked value while keeping the business record. The walkthrough shows how to build the policy, apply it to the customer’s Contact, and review the change. The same request process can use a policy configured to delete records instead.

Build the reusable RTBF policy

For a retention example, first confirm that retention is available in the org and prepare the Data Store and access described in Vignette 5.

  1. Open Home → Right to Be Forgotten Policies → New, and choose Right to Be Forgotten Policy. Name it clearly, for example CRM Contact RTBF-V3.
  2. Click Select Object, choose Contact, and continue. The population filter is disabled because the request supplies the target records.
  3. Under Data In Org, choose Mask. Set Email to Replace with Static Value, using mask-rtbf-policy@privacy-center.com for the pictured example. Leave Unique off and unrelated fields as Do Not Change.
  4. Configure history and file handling only where required for the treatment. The basic example preserves history for the before-and-after view.
  5. Under Data Store Handling, select Do Not Copy for this example. Choose Copy to Store and the required fields and duration when retention is needed; see Vignette 5.
  6. Click Done. To include related processing objects, use Active Objects → Add Child Object beside the completed parent card, then configure those objects.
  7. Save the object and policy changes, then Publish.

Choose what happens to the record

Choose Mask to keep the record and replace selected fields. Choose Delete to remove the record. Configure the related records, history, files, and any retained copy as part of the policy.

The request and the policy have different jobs
The request identifies the person. The policy defines which records and fields to process and whether to mask, delete, or retain them.
Apply it to a person's request

When you already have the Contact ID

The policy is ready. Now choose whose records it applies to. Use this route when you already have the person's Contact record ID and a published RTBF policy.

  1. Open the disposable Contact and note its starting Email.
  2. From Privacy Center Home, open Right to Be Forgotten Requests. Click New RTBF Request to open the guided request form.
  3. Paste the Contact ID into Record to Delete, choose the published RTBF policy, and click Submit.
  4. Open the request that was created and click Run Now. Follow its progress in the associated Privacy Job Session.
  5. When processing completes, refresh the Contact and review the Email and matching Contact History entry.
Why the form says Record to Delete
This field identifies the Contact. The policy you select determines whether its data is masked or deleted.

When you need to find the person first

Use this route when you have a name or email and need to find the person's records before choosing what to process.

  1. Configure Privacy Request Settings for the intended CRM search fields and objects.
  2. Open the Privacy Requests tab and click New Privacy Request. Enter the person's name or other configured search details and save. On Details, scroll to the privacy search component and click Search.
  3. Select the matching Contact result. Under Record Handling, choose the published RTBF policy. Exclude results that should remain unchanged.
  4. Select Submit Requests, review the summary, and confirm Submit.
  5. On the same Details page, open Related Requests and then Included Records. Follow the CRM job and review the result on the Contact.

Both routes can use the same masking or deletion policy for CRM data. The search route can also find Data 360 records, as shown in Vignette 3.

Data Subject Requests · Vignette 3

3. Data 360 Right to Be Forgotten

Overview

A person’s information can be present both in Salesforce CRM and in Data 360, where an Individual record represents that person. Privacy Center can search both locations from one Privacy Request and let the team choose what happens to each result. CRM records are processed using an RTBF policy; the Data 360 deletion is sent through its Consent API and processed in Data 360.

Use case

The privacy team needs to find a customer’s records in both systems and submit a deletion for their Data 360 information. This walkthrough searches for the person, leaves the CRM Contact unchanged, and submits the Data 360 Individual for deletion. The team then uses the Individual ID to check the result in Data Explorer.

Set up the person search
  1. In Data 360, open Data Explorer and find the test Individual. Note its Individual ID, source, and data space.
  2. Open Setup → Privacy Request Settings → Search Criteria. Select the search inputs; the example uses first name, last name, and email.
  3. Under Salesforce Org Objects, select Contact and save. Complete this CRM configuration before saving the Data Cloud search settings.
  4. Under Data Cloud Objects, enable Search Data Cloud Objects and click Edit Fields. Select Individual (ssot__Individual__dlm), include it in searches, and choose First Name, Last Name, and Individual ID.
  5. Save the field selection and the overall settings. Confirm the operator's Data 360 and data-space access.
Find the person and submit the deletion
  1. Open Privacy Requests → New Privacy Request, enter the person's search criteria, and save.
  2. On Details, scroll to the privacy component and click Search.
  3. For the Data 360-only example, set the CRM Contact to Exclude Record from Request and the intended Data Cloud Individual to Delete this Record.
  4. Click Submit Requests, review the included records, and confirm Submit once.
  5. Open Details → Related Requests → Included Records to follow the submitted targets.
  6. After processing, use Data Explorer to search for that Individual ID and review the result in Data 360.

Proactive Organizational Data Management · Vignette 4

4. Data Management: lifecycle masking

Overview

Organizations accumulate records they no longer need for everyday work, such as inactive customer Contacts. A Data Management Policy finds records using rules set by the organization and either deletes them or masks selected personal details. This lets the team apply the same cleanup rule repeatedly, rather than searching for old records and changing them manually. When a separate copy is required, retention can be added to the policy.

Use case

The organization no longer needs email addresses for a group of inactive Contacts, but it still needs the Contact records for business reference. In this example, the Inactive checkbox identifies that group. In a real deployment, the organization first agrees what makes a record ready for cleanup—for example, the end of a customer relationship followed by an agreed period—and uses those conditions in the policy. The team previews the expected impact before changing anything.

Build the reusable cleanup policy

For a retention example, first confirm that retention is available in the org and prepare the Data Store and access described in Vignette 5.

  1. Create disposable Contacts with known Email values and set the custom Inactive checkbox.
  2. Open Home → Data Management Policies → New → Data Management Policy. Enter a name and add Contact.
  3. Set the filter to Inactive = True and add any condition needed to isolate the exercise. The pictured policy also filters on Email.
  4. Choose Data In Org → Mask. For Email, select Replace with Static Value, using masked-inactive@privacy-center.com in this example. Leave Unique off and other fields as Do Not Change.
  5. In Data Store Handling, choose Do Not Copy for the masking-only example and click Done.
  6. To include related processing objects, return to Active Objects, scroll to the Contact card, and click Add Child Object. Configure those objects' treatments. Add Object adds another top-level processing object.
  7. Save the completed policy configuration.
Navigation checkpoint: add related records
Finish the Contact wizard with Done. Return to Active Objects and scroll below the default retention controls to the Contact card. Add Child Object is on the right. Add Cross-Object Query inside the filter is a different control: it selects which Contacts qualify.

The value of a lifecycle policy

Updating or deleting a field is only part of the work. Someone also has to decide which records qualify, check the impact, schedule the work, and review the results. A Data Management Policy gives the team a repeatable way to manage those steps together.

Preview, publish, and run it

The policy is ready. Now let's check which records match before we run it.

  1. Show the starting Contact values, the filter, and the Email treatment.
  2. Click Generate Preview → Generate. Stay on Preview while the asynchronous preview completes.
  3. Review the expected matched, retained, deleted, and masked counts. Adjust the policy and regenerate the preview when the scope needs correction.
  4. Click Acknowledge, then Publish.
  5. Select the published policy's Run Now action. Follow the actual, non-preview Privacy Job Session.
  6. Review counts and errors, then refresh the same Contact and inspect the masked Email and history.

Three separate configuration controls

Adding objects and filtering records
Control Purpose
Add Object on Active Objects Add a top-level processing object with its own configuration.
Add Child Object beside Contact Include related records as processing targets with their own treatment.
Add Cross-Object Query in Contact's filter Use related-record conditions to select which Contacts qualify. The query itself does not process those related records.

For example, select Contacts based on a Case condition is a filtering decision. Mask or delete related Cases as well is a child-object treatment decision.

Cross-object filter queries are limited to one level down and do not support polymorphic relationship fields. Those restrictions apply to filtering; they do not define the number of child processing objects in a policy.

Optional retention · Vignette 5

5. Data Store and external-object access

Overview

Sometimes an organization no longer needs personal information in its everyday Salesforce records but still needs to keep selected information for a defined period. A CRM RTBF or Data Management policy can retain those values in a separate Privacy Center store while masking or deleting the live data. Salesforce Connect lets authorized users view the retained records from within Salesforce. This section explains that optional storage and access setup.

Use case

The organization masks email addresses on inactive Contacts but needs to keep selected pre-mask values separately. An authorized user must be able to review those retained records without restoring the original email addresses to the live Contacts. This walkthrough sets up the store connection and a Retained Contacts tab for that purpose.

Set up retention and access to the store

Retention is part of the policy—not another request you have to run. Use this setup when a CRM RTBF or Data Management policy also needs to keep selected data separately. The screenshots show the store connection and access to retained records. Continue to use the request or cleanup process in Vignette 2 or Vignette 4 to run the policy.

How the pieces fit

Retention components and their roles
Component Role
CRM RTBF or Data Management policy Selects the records and defines live-record treatment, retained fields, and retention duration. Policy execution creates the retained copy.
Privacy Center Data Store Holds the selected retained data separately from the operational record.
Salesforce Connect external data source Connects Salesforce to the managed retention store.
External object and tab Provides the authorized in-org view of retained rows, such as Retained Contacts.

Policy execution → retained copy → Salesforce Connect → external-object view

Prepare the store

  1. Confirm that the PrivacyCenterRetention capability and store availability are provisioned for the org.
  2. Add Data Stores to the Privacy Center app when needed. Open Data Stores → Create Data Store and establish the store for the exercise.
  3. On the CRM RTBF or Data Management policy, choose Copy to Store, the retention period, and field handling such as Email → Copy Value. Set unnecessary fields to Do Not Copy.
  4. Run the CRM RTBF or Data Management policy to copy the selected values into the store. The following connection setup lets authorized users view those retained records.

6. Privacy Hold: design example

Overview

Some records must be kept even when they would normally qualify for cleanup, such as information needed for an investigation. A Privacy Hold records why the Contact must be preserved. This design uses an Active Privacy Hold checkbox on Contact, kept up to date by automation, so the cleanup policy can skip protected records. The cleanup still runs; the policy leaves the held records alone.

Use case

Two Contacts are inactive, but one is needed for an ongoing investigation. The organization wants to leave that Contact unchanged while masking the other Contact’s Email. The design records the hold, keeps the Contact’s hold indicator up to date, and checks that the cleanup policy selects only the unprotected Contact.

Configure the policy to skip held records

Rule: process inactive Contacts only when no qualifying active Privacy Hold protects them. The Contact's Active Privacy Hold checkbox gives the cleanup policy a simple way to check.

Hold records and business logic

  1. Open Privacy Hold Reasons → New and define a reason, such as Legal Investigation.
  2. Open Privacy Holds → New. Set the Contact reference, reason, active status, and relevant dates.
  3. Prepare a held Contact and an unheld control. Both can have Contact.Inactive = True; only the held Contact has a qualifying active hold.
  4. Define which active statuses and date rules make a hold qualifying for the lifecycle policy.

Keep the Active Privacy Hold checkbox up to date

Create a Contact checkbox called Active Privacy Hold (for example, Active_Privacy_Hold__c). Use Flow, Apex, or another authorized automation to keep it up to date: check it when at least one qualifying active hold applies to the Contact, and clear it only when none remain.

Process Contact when:
  Inactive = True
  AND Active_Privacy_Hold__c = False

Whenever a hold is created, released, moved, or deleted, the automation checks all qualifying holds for the affected Contact. Releasing one hold must leave the checkbox checked while another active hold remains. If a hold moves to a different Contact, update the checkbox on both Contacts.

Selection and treatment

The Contact can be inactive and still be under an active hold. Inactive identifies a candidate for cleanup; Active Privacy Hold tells the policy to leave that Contact alone.

In a cross-object filtering design, Is Active = True identifies qualifying related holds and Result Rule = False expresses exclusion of the parent when a match exists. Check relationship support before choosing that filtering route; the derived-indicator design keeps eligibility on Contact itself.

Run the cleanup and check the exception
  1. Show the held and unheld Contacts and their Active Privacy Hold checkboxes.
  2. Open the lifecycle policy and explain Inactive = True AND Active_Privacy_Hold__c = False.
  3. Generate a preview and confirm that the held Contact is excluded and the unheld control is included.
  4. Run the controlled treatment and compare both records afterward.
Checks for the hold design

Verify the preservation rule

Use these checks before allowing the lifecycle policy to process records. They cover both the records selected by the policy and the automation that maintains the checkbox.

Privacy Hold design checks
Test case Expected result
Inactive Contact; no active hold The checkbox is clear. The Contact is included by the controlled lifecycle filter.
Inactive Contact; one active hold The checkbox is checked. The Contact is excluded.
Two active holds; release one The checkbox stays checked. The Contact remains protected.
Release the final qualifying hold The checkbox clears after the automation confirms no qualifying holds remain.
Move or delete a hold Recalculate the affected old and new references as applicable.
Active-date rules change Recalculate eligibility according to the configured date and status rules.
The checkbox may be out of date Pause the cleanup and check the automation before continuing.

Keep the exception without stopping cleanup

A hold tells the cleanup process, “Leave this record alone; it is still needed.” In this design, the Contact's hold checkbox carries that instruction into the policy, so other eligible records can still be processed. If more than one hold applies, ending one does not release the record while another remains active.

Consent capture and centralized management

Preference Manager lets people express their choices. Global Consent Manager gives the organization a central place to manage consent information.

Preference Manager and Global Consent Manager
Capability Role
Preference Manager Build and publish customer-facing preference forms. Choose the template, hosting, identity, and mapped Salesforce fields or consent records.
Global Consent Manager Maintain a centralized, attribute-based consent system of record, using reusable consent definitions and an auditable history of consent changes.

Global Consent Manager concepts

A Global Consent Definition is a reusable set of information to collect for a consent use case. For example, define the details needed for a communication preference once and use that definition again for similar interactions.

Attributes hold details such as Consent Status and Consent Purpose. Add other attributes when the organization needs to record more information.

A customer may want product updates by email but not promotional texts. A single “yes” or “no” does not capture that distinction. The consent record needs to say what the choice applies to, and the systems using it need to know where to find the current answer.

Guided consent layouts arrange those details on a person's record. Communication Consent covers messaging choices; Action Consent covers uses of data, such as sharing or AI processing.

Consent history records changes so teams can review how consent was granted or withdrawn.

What happens after someone changes a preference?

When someone opts out of marketing emails, four things need to happen: give them a clear way to make the choice, record it against the right person, make it available to the systems sending those emails, and have those systems act on it. The form starts the process; the outcome is that the marketing emails stop.

Four aspects of consent
AspectIn an everyday example
Collection experienceCapture the choice. A customer uses a form to say, “Stop sending me marketing emails.”
Identity-linked recordRemember who chose what. Record the choice against the right person and make clear that it applies to marketing emails, not necessarily every kind of communication.
Signal distributionMake the choice available. The system sending marketing emails receives the update or checks the current preference.
EnforcementAct on the choice. The sending process excludes that customer from the relevant marketing emails.

The customer sees one choice. We need all four parts to work.

The Basic form in this walkthrough captures the choice and updates Email Opt Out on Contact. Global Consent Manager provides a central consent model for a broader solution. The implementation then connects that record to the systems using the choice, so they can receive or check the current answer and act on it.

How does the sending system get the latest preference, and where does it check that preference before sending? Does it receive an update when the choice changes, or look up the current answer? What happens if an update fails?

8. Agentforce in Privacy Center

Overview

Agentforce in Privacy Center helps a privacy team decide what needs attention in its Salesforce environment. Org checks identify setup gaps, such as fields missing classification tags. Compliance scans compare the organization’s setup and policies with rules derived from its selected compliance framework. The team can review each finding, consider the recommended fix, and follow the resulting actions in the Audit Log.

Use case

The privacy team wants to know what needs attention first. Start with a specific finding: a field is missing its classification tag, or an object has no retention duration. Open the issue, understand why it was raised, and review the proposed fix. The team can then choose the appropriate action and follow what happened in the Audit Log.

Set up the compliance framework
  1. Open App Launcher → Agentforce in Privacy Center.
  2. Confirm the required Privacy Center, AI, and Data 360 setup. Assign Manage Privacy Agent or View Privacy Agent according to the user's role.
  3. Select the countries and regulatory frameworks relevant to the organization. This example uses Canada and PIPEDA. Save the framework and allow its compliance rules to generate.
  4. Run the Org Scan and then the Compliance Scan from the setup assistant. Complete the audit-access check and finish setup.
Run scans, review findings, and take action
  1. Open the dashboard. Run the Org Scan and Compliance Scan as needed, or review the existing results. Check the Org Check Issues and Compliance Issues counts and the last-run times.
  2. Open an org-check issue, such as a field missing its classification tag or a missing retention duration.
  3. Open a compliance issue. Read the related rule, the affected object, and the suggested change.
  4. Click Resolve to review the solution. Select Apply Solution when ready to apply the proposed action, or Ignore Issue when appropriate.
  5. Open the Audit Log to review the actions taken.

Scans and what they detect

The selected compliance framework generates rules for the organization. For example, a rule might require personal information to have a retention policy. These rules help Agentforce identify gaps and recommend changes. The two scans answer different questions.

Two AI conversations: helping privacy and using personal information

How can AI help our privacy team?

Here, AI helps the privacy team work through its requirements: identify a gap, explain why it matters, suggest a fix, and let the team decide what to do. After the selected action runs, the team can review what happened. That is the story behind the scans, findings, proposed solutions, and Audit Log in this walkthrough.

Requirements → checks → findings → recommendations → reviewed action → audit history.

What happens to personal information when our business uses AI?

This is a different conversation. We need to understand which personal information the AI uses—whether to train a model or to answer a particular question—what appears in the answer, and what is kept afterward. Consider an assistant that reads customer case notes and produces a summary: the privacy discussion needs to cover the notes, the summary, and any saved conversation, not just the original customer record.

Depending on the application, the team may need to examine source records, data prepared for training or retrieval, search indexes or embeddings, the model, prompts and context, generated answers, and logs or caches. These are places to examine in the AI design, not a single sequence that every application follows.

AI can help us manage privacy. We still need to manage the personal information our AI uses.

Discussion and planning

Workshop: from the demo to your organization

Use these questions and examples to connect the walkthroughs to your organization. Start with a recent request or cleanup, look at where the work slows down, and agree which improvement would matter most. Use your own figures for the measures below.

Questions

Economics. What does it cost your team to complete a data subject access request today? How much time goes into finding the information, reviewing it, and chasing other teams?

Auditability. After a deletion, how would you show what was requested, what ran, and whether it succeeded, without keeping personal information that should have been removed?

Architecture. When your scripts or internal workflows process a large backlog, such as millions of legacy records, how often do actions fail or need to be rerun? Who investigates the errors and maintains the process?

Legal. How does the legal team make sure an active litigation or investigation hold reaches every cleanup process that could change or delete the protected records? What happens when a hold ends?

Consent. When someone opts out on your website, how long does it take before the marketing system stops including them? How do you check that the change reached it?

Security. Before giving a privacy vendor API access to Salesforce, what do you check about its permissions, the data it retrieves, where that data goes, and how long it keeps it?

AI readiness. Before personal information is used to train an AI model or help it answer a question, what checks keep unclassified information or records past their agreed retention period out of that workload? Who decides which data is appropriate, and which checks are automated?

Metrics & Considerations

Value

Managed capabilities

The work is bigger than changing a field. Someone has to maintain the rules, review the expected impact, run the work, and follow up on errors. Privacy Center puts policy configuration, lifecycle previews, scheduling, and job visibility into an administered process rather than leaving the team to assemble every part itself.

Templated activities

Build the instructions once, then reuse them. A Portability Policy defines what to collect for an access request; a CRM RTBF policy defines what to mask, delete, or retain. The next request supplies the person's record rather than starting another policy-building exercise.

Fewer avoidable manual errors

Look for the steps that depend on someone remembering a relationship, repeating an export, or chasing the next team. Reusable policies can reduce that repeated work and leave people more time to review exceptions. Measure the improvement in your own process rather than assuming automation removes every mistake.

Auditability

After the data changes, the team should still be able to explain what happened. Keep the request, policy, job reference, outcome, and relevant approvals together under an agreed evidence-retention approach. Privacy Job Sessions provide execution details and errors; the wider process determines what evidence is retained and for how long.

The work around custom scripts

A script or Flow may be a good fit for a small, stable job. At larger scale, discuss who handles batching, retries, record locks, changing relationships, and result checks. Compare that ongoing work with the policy and job-management capabilities available in Privacy Center, rather than assuming custom code will fail or that a product will never encounter errors.

External API access to Salesforce

Ask what an external privacy tool needs to read, change, or copy out of Salesforce, and why. Performing the Salesforce treatment through native policies can avoid exporting records solely to transform them elsewhere. The wider design still needs appropriate access, data handling, and controls for exports and integrations.

AI governance

Decide what personal information an AI use case should receive before supplying it for training or retrieval. Source records, generated answers, saved conversations, and models need their own handling decisions. Privacy Center can help reduce unnecessary Salesforce data, and Agentforce can highlight privacy configuration gaps; the AI application still needs controls that apply those decisions to its own data use.

Retention storage

We may need to keep selected information without keeping it in everyday use. CRM RTBF and Data Management policies can retain chosen values separately while masking or deleting the live data. Decide what to keep, who can view it, and how long it remains.

Dependency management

One person can have information across Contacts, Cases, Opportunities, Tasks, and other records. The policy needs the relationships and fields that belong in the request. Defining that scope makes collection and treatment repeatable; it is not the same as automatically discovering every record about the person across every system.

Coexistence with enterprise platforms

Your existing privacy platform may already be the right place to receive requests and coordinate teams. Privacy Center can be the configured Salesforce part of that process. For an access request, the documented Portability API runs an active policy and returns information for retrieving the compiled data file. Deletion needs its corresponding request workflow. The integration must connect the right action and return its outcome; submitting a request is not the same as confirming it completed.

Archiving, backups, and privacy retention

Start with the job you need done. Recovery after data loss, longer-term access to historical records, retaining selected personal information, and preserving records for an investigation are related but different needs. Privacy Center's retention option keeps selected values as part of RTBF or Data Management processing. Existing backup, archive, or legal-preservation tools may still have their own roles.
